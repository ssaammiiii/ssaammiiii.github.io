SignSpeak

Deaf patients in Ghana often arrive at a clinic with no interpreter. SignSpeak is a pair of sensor gloves that recognise Ghanaian Sign Language and speak the result aloud, so a patient and a clinician can hold a basic conversation on their own. The gloves read hand movement directly, so nothing is filmed in the consulting room and the system works without raising any privacy concerns. I co-developed it with one teammate as my undergraduate thesis, supervised by Dr. Nii Longdon Sowah. Code and documentation on GitHub.

Two gloves

Five flex sensors, an IMU and hand-made contact pads per glove, so look-alike letters such as A/T and U/V stay apart.

24 channels at 50 Hz

ESP32 board

Firmware filters, calibrates and streams the data, and pairs frames from the two gloves' independent clocks.

Firmware in C/C++

Classifier

A shrinkage LDA model on a laptop labels each window in 0.13 ms, matching a BiLSTM at about 1,500 times the speed.

26 letters + 11 word signs

Spoken out loud

An LLM turns the recognised signs into a sentence and text-to-speech voices it, about two seconds from gesture to audio.

Sign to speech in ~2 s

Tested on signers it had never seen

Clinical word signs
92%
Average accuracy across three new signers who were not in the training data. The training set is 1,864 repetitions from two signers; on a held-out split the letter model reaches 99.2%. Parts for a pair of gloves cost about US$135.

Camera-only prototype

A webcam baseline using MediaPipe hand tracking and OpenCV recognises the same signs with nothing on the hand, for a paired comparison with the gloves.

S. Soleimani, I. Bhardwaj, N. L. Sowah. "SignSpeak: A Low-Cost Sensor Glove for Fingerspelling and Clinical Sign Recognition, with a Paired Camera Baseline." Manuscript in preparation, 2026.
